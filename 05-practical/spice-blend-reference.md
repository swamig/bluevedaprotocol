# Spice Blend Master Reference

The house blends the protocol's recipes call for, encoded once so every sheet can point here instead of restating ratios. Covers **garam masala** (the warming aromatic finish) and **chaat masala** (the tangy-savory souring finish) — two blends that do opposite jobs and should never be mixed. Each recipe is written **self-contained**: every step restates its own amounts so you never scroll back to an ingredient table mid-cook.

Companion to [Bhuna Masala Cube (Sheet 39)](./bhuna-masala-cube.md), [Kadhi Reference (Sheet 40)](./kadhi-reference.md), and [Black Beans (samosa spice split)](./protocol-blackbeans.md) — all of which call for one or both of these blends.

---

## The Two-Blend Mental Model

| Blend | Job | Goes in | Built around |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Garam masala** | Warm + aromatic finish | OFF-HEAT at the end (volatiles burn above ~200°F) | Cinnamon · clove · cardamom · cumin · coriander · pepper |
| **Chaat masala** | Sour + bright + savory finish | At the end, like a dry lemon | **Amchur** (dried mango) · black salt · cumin |

**They are not interchangeable.** Garam masala adds warmth and depth; chaat masala adds tang and a sulfurous savory punch. Amchur belongs in chaat masala — **never in garam masala**, where its sourness would muddy every dish you finish with the blend.

## 1. Garam Masala (from scratch)

The full canonical blend. Toast whole, grind fresh — it beats any jar by a wide margin because pre-ground garam masala loses ~50% of its volatile compounds within 6 months of bottling, even unopened.

### Yield + storage

| Output | Value |
|---|---|
| Yield | ~½ cup ground |
| Storage | Airtight glass jar · cool dark cupboard |
| Peak quality | **6 months** · still usable to ~1 year |
| Covers | ~20+ single bhuna batches, OR daily sprinkles on dal/yogurt/sabzi for months |

### Method (~15 min · self-contained — amounts restated each step)

1. **Gather the toast group.** Measure into one bowl: coriander seeds **3 tbsp** · cumin seeds **2 tbsp** · green cardamom pods **2 tbsp (~20-25 pods)** · black peppercorns **1 tbsp** · Ceylon cinnamon **2 sticks (~3″ each, broken into pieces)** · whole cloves **1 tbsp (~30 cloves)** · black cardamom **3 pods** · bay leaves **3** · star anise **1 whole (optional)**. Hold back nutmeg + mace — they're added at the grind, not the toast.
2. **Dry toast.** Heat a dry skillet on **medium-low** (no oil) · add the entire toast group · toast **3-5 min**, stirring/shaking constantly, until fragrant and one shade darker. **Cue:** cumin + coriander turn light brown, faint crackling, big aroma release. **Stop at fragrant, not dark** — the moment they smoke you've gone too far.
3. **Cool.** Tip onto a plate · cool **5 min completely**. Grinding warm spices makes a paste, not a powder.
4. **Crack the cardamom.** Split the green **(2 tbsp)** + black **(3 pods)** cardamom · discard the husks · keep only the seeds.
5. **Grind.** Add all toasted spices + cardamom seeds to a spice grinder. Grate in fresh nutmeg **½ whole nut** + add mace **1 tsp (optional)**. Grind in 20-30 sec bursts until fine.
6. **Sift (optional).** Sift for ultra-fine powder · re-grind the coarse bits left in the sieve.
7. **Store.** Airtight glass jar · label with the date · cool dark cupboard · **6-month peak**.

### Quick version (pre-ground · 5 min · no whole spices)

Lower aroma but serviceable. **Don't toast pre-ground spices** — they scorch instantly. Just whisk together:

| Pre-ground spice | Amount |
|---|---|
| Ground coriander | 2 tbsp |
| Ground cumin | 1 tbsp |
| Ground cardamom | 2 tsp |
| Ground black pepper | 1 tbsp |
| Ground cinnamon | 1 tbsp |
| Ground cloves | 1 tsp |
| Ground nutmeg | 1 tsp |

Keeps ~3 months before the aroma fades (vs 6 for the toasted whole-spice version).

### DIY from whatever singles you have (fallback ladder)

When you don't have the full whole-spice lineup, build the blend from what's on the rack — ordered best to most minimal:

| What you have | Use (per ~4 tsp blend equivalent) |
|---|---|
| Cinnamon + cardamom + cloves + nutmeg | 1 tsp cinnamon + 1 tsp cardamom + ½ tsp cloves + ½ tsp nutmeg + 1 tsp extra black pepper — closest match |
| Cinnamon + cloves + nutmeg (no cardamom) | 1½ tsp cinnamon + ½ tsp cloves + ½ tsp nutmeg + 1 tsp extra black pepper |
| Cinnamon + cloves only | 2 tsp cinnamon + 1 tsp cloves + 1 tsp extra black pepper |
| Cinnamon + nutmeg only | 2 tsp cinnamon + 1 tsp nutmeg + 1 tsp extra black pepper |
| Cinnamon only | 2 tsp cinnamon + 1 tsp extra black pepper + 1 tsp extra cumin |
| None of the above | Skip garam masala · add 1 extra tsp kasuri methi instead — you lose the warm bouquet but the dish still works |

### Regional dialing

| Style | Tweak |
|---|---|
| **Punjabi (default above)** | Clove + cardamom forward · the standard North Indian blend |
| **Kashmiri** | Add 1 tsp fennel + ½ tsp ginger powder · skip the black cardamom heat · more aromatic, less sharp |
| **South Indian** | Add 1 tsp fennel + 4-5 dried curry leaves toasted in |
| **Maharashtrian (goda-lean)** | Add 1 tbsp sesame + 1 tsp dagad phool (stone flower) + a touch of dried coconut |

### Why fresh-grind matters (the protocol case)

- Pre-ground jar garam masala loses **~50% of volatile compounds within 6 months** of bottling — even unopened.
- The toast step generates Maillard browning + volatilizes the essential oils right before grinding = peak aroma.
- For the cube system specifically: garam masala goes in **OFF-HEAT** at the end of the bhuna ([Sheet 39 Step 7](./bhuna-masala-cube.md)) precisely because these volatiles are fragile. A fresh-ground blend there maximizes the finishing bouquet that survives into the freeze.

### Amounts for the protocol's recipes

| Recipe | Garam masala |
|---|---|
| [Bhuna Masala Cube](./bhuna-masala-cube.md) single batch | 1 tsp |
| Bhuna Masala Cube 4× Vitamix batch | 4 tsp |
| [Kadhi](./kadhi-reference.md) (Punjabi) | not standard — kadhi uses tadka, not garam masala |
| Daily sprinkle on dal / yogurt / sabzi | ⅛-¼ tsp at the end |

## 2. Chaat Masala (where amchur lives)

The tangy-savory souring blend. Built around **amchur** (dried green-mango powder) for tartness and **black salt (kala namak)** for the sulfurous savory punch. This is amchur's true home — NOT garam masala.

### Yield + storage

| Output | Value |
|---|---|
| Yield | ~½ cup ground |
| Storage | Airtight glass jar · cool dark cupboard |
| Peak quality | **4-6 months** (amchur clumps with humidity — add a few rice grains to the jar to keep it dry) |

### Method (~10 min · self-contained)

1. **Toast the seeds.** Heat a dry skillet on medium-low · add cumin seeds **3 tbsp** + coriander seeds **1 tbsp** · toast 60-90 sec until fragrant + one shade darker · cool 3 min. (Toast these two only — the rest of the blend is added ground, untoasted.)
2. **Grind the toasted seeds** to a fine powder in a spice grinder or mortar.
3. **Combine off-heat.** In a bowl, whisk the ground toasted seeds with: amchur (dried mango powder) **3 tbsp** · black salt / kala namak **2 tbsp** · regular salt **1 tsp** · ground ginger **1 tsp** · black pepper **1 tsp** · Kashmiri chili powder **1 tsp** · ajwain (carom), ground **½ tsp** · asafoetida (hing) **¼ tsp** · dried mint, crushed **1 tsp (optional)**.
4. **Store.** Airtight glass jar + a few rice grains (humidity guard) · label with date · **4-6 month peak**.

### Where chaat masala + amchur go

| Use | How |
|---|---|
| Chaats, fruit, raita | ¼-½ tsp sprinkled at the end |
| Finishing tang on a sabzi / channa that needs brightness | ¼-½ tsp — works like a dry lemon |
| Purple sweet potato chaat base | per [freeze-system day-of additions](./freeze-system.md) |
| Roasted chickpea croutons | toss while warm |

### Amchur alone (without the full chaat blend)

If a recipe just wants tang, use **amchur straight** — ¼-½ tsp at the end of cooking, like a dry lemon. The protocol already uses straight amchur in:

- [Black bean samosa filling](./protocol-blackbeans.md) (added now, batch-stable)
- [Bhuna Masala — Marwari variant](./bhuna-masala-cube.md) (a pinch added to the masala at the spice-bloom step, NOT routed through garam masala)

## 3. Why Amchur Is Not in Garam Masala

The single most common spice-blend mistake. The reasoning, once, so it's settled:

- **Garam masala is a warming aromatic blend.** Its job is depth + warmth + fragrance. Every ingredient is a warm aromatic (cinnamon, clove, cardamom) or an earthy base (cumin, coriander, pepper).
- **Amchur is a souring agent.** Its only job is tart acidity. It has no aromatic warmth to contribute.
- **Mixed into garam masala, amchur muddies the blend** — you'd taste a faint sourness in *everything* you finish with it, including dishes that want pure warmth (most curries, dals, the bhuna base). It also shortens shelf life (amchur is hygroscopic and clumps).
- **Keep them as separate jars.** Warm + aromatic in one (garam masala, off-heat at the end). Sour + bright in another (chaat masala / straight amchur, as a finishing tang where the dish wants acid).

## 4. Self-Contained Step Format (why these recipes restate amounts)

Every method in this sheet (and in [Sheet 39 Bhuna](./bhuna-masala-cube.md)) restates its ingredient amounts inline at each step rather than forcing you to scroll back to a top-of-page ingredient table mid-cook. The convention:

- **Single-batch amount first**, scale amount in **bold parentheses** after where relevant — e.g. "cumin seeds **1 tsp (4× 4 tsp)**"
- **Volume + count both given** where they diverge — e.g. "16 onions / ~16 cups raw → ~5 cups after pulse"
- **Held-out / off-heat ingredients flagged inline** at the step they're added, not buried in a separate list

This is the protocol's standard for any multi-step recipe going forward — the ingredient table stays as a shopping reference, but the method is executable standalone.
