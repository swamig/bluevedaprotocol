# Red Chili Management

The single reference for red chilies in the protocol — fresh and dried. Covers sourcing, freeze-on-arrival rule, bug + mold inspection (with the honest freezer temperature reality), rehydration method, dried-to-fresh conversions, and where each chili type lands across the cube system.

Companion to [Sheet 23 — Homemade Sriracha](./sriracha-homemade.md) and [Sheet 37 — Fermented Chili-Garlic Master Paste](./fermented-chili-garlic-paste.md). Both reference this sheet for dried-chili swaps and inspection rules.

## 2. Fresh Chili Sourcing

| Lever | Spec |
|---|---|
| Freshness signal | Firm skin, no soft patches, taut stem, no shriveling |
| Color | Fully red — green-and-red mixed = unripe (lower capsaicin) |
| Size | Medium uniform (~3" Fresno) — easier to weigh-batch consistently |
| Source | High-turnover store · Mexican / Indian / SE Asian groceries beat big-box |
| Bulk | 1-2 lb per Sriracha + master paste session — buy by the pound, not by the chili |
| Cost target | $4-8 per lb Fresno / red jalapeño · Thai red runs higher per ounce |
| Avoid | Pre-bagged "rainbow chili mix" — heat varies per piece, ferment results unpredictable |

### Fresh storage

| Storage | Life |
|---|---|
| **Fridge crisper, paper bag** (NOT plastic — traps moisture, accelerates rot) | 1-2 weeks |
| Fridge, sealed glass container with paper towel layer | 2-3 weeks |
| Freezer, whole + bagged (no blanch needed) | 6 months — texture mush on thaw but fine for ferment/cooking |
| Counter | Same day only — use within 24 hr or refrigerate |

## 3. Dried Chili Sourcing

| Lever | Spec |
|---|---|
| Freshness signal | Flexible, not brittle · color vivid (bright red, not dusty brown) · clean smell |
| Avoid | Brittle (cracks when bent = too old) · dusty bag · musty smell · visible holes or webbing |
| Source | Indian or Mexican grocery beats supermarket spice aisle 10:1 on price + freshness |
| Cost target | Kashmiri $4-8 per ½ lb · Thai bird's eye similar · supermarket $1/oz is overpriced |
| Bulk | Buy small (½ lb) and use within 6 months — bulk is bug-magnet territory |
| Bag check | Hold bag up to light — look for moving things, webbing, frass piles, holes |

### Dried storage

| Storage | Life |
|---|---|
| **Airtight glass jar at room temp** (after freeze-on-arrival — see §5) | 6-12 months peak |
| Original plastic bag from store | **2 months** — bugs chew through plastic |
| Fridge (sealed glass) | 12+ months · slows oxidation of capsaicinoids |
| Freezer (sealed glass or freezer bag) | 2+ years · best for bulk holding |

## 4. Inspection — Bug + Mold Triage

### Bug signs (very common in dried chilies, especially imported)

| Sign | Severity | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Live moving bugs | High | Toss that chili · inspect rest of batch carefully |
| Webbing (silk threads) inside cavity | High | Toss that chili · pantry moth larvae · spreads |
| Frass (dark specks like fine sand) in cavity | Medium-high | Toss that chili · bug poop, eggs likely present |
| Drilled holes through wall | Medium-high | Toss that chili · beetle entry/exit · eggs likely |
| Suspicious dust (musty vs chili-dust) | Medium | Sniff — musty = toss, just dusty = salvageable |
| Otherwise clean appearance | Low risk | Salvageable but **assume eggs are present** — freeze before storing |
| ≥50% of batch shows damage | Whole bag | Toss the entire bag · contaminated past salvage |

### Mold signs — HARD RULE

| Sign | Action |
|---|---|
| Any visible fuzz, discoloration patch, or "powdery white" patch on chili | **Toss that chili.** Mold has already penetrated flesh — mycotoxins are heat-stable, acid-stable, ferment-stable. **Cannot be washed off.** |
| Multiple chilies in bag show visible mold | **Toss entire bag.** Spores have spread invisibly to others. |
| Musty, damp, mildewy smell (not chili smell) | **Toss entire bag.** Likely aflatoxin / ochratoxin contamination. |
| Chilies clumped together by sticky substance | **Toss entire bag.** Mold mycelium binding them. |

**Why this is non-negotiable:** Aflatoxins (from *Aspergillus flavus*) and ochratoxins (from *A. ochraceus*) are Group 1 carcinogens. They survive cooking, freezing, fermenting, and any wash. They penetrate the flesh long before visible mold appears. A $5 bag of dried chilies is never worth gambling on this.

## 5. Freeze-On-Arrival Rule

**Every new bag of dried chilies goes straight to the freezer for 72 hr before pantry storage** — kills bugs at every life stage (adults, larvae, eggs). Then transfer to airtight glass jars for pantry storage.

This is the single biggest preventive measure against pantry-pest infestation. Most "clean-looking" dried chilies have at least eggs present from the source warehouse.

### Honest freezer temperature reality

Most home freezers do NOT run at 0°F / -18°C unless explicitly verified. Typical functional temps in the wild:

| Freezer type | Actual temp range | Egg-kill time needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Chest freezer** (purpose-built deep freeze) | -10 to 0°F / -23 to -18°C | 72 hr ✓ |
| **Top-mount fridge-freezer** (standard kitchen freezer) | 0 to 10°F / -18 to -12°C | 72 hr - 7 days, depends on actual temp |
| **Side-by-side fridge-freezer** | 5 to 15°F / -15 to -9°C | 5-7 days minimum |
| **French-door bottom freezer** | 0 to 10°F / -18 to -12°C | 72 hr - 7 days |
| **Mini-fridge ice compartment** | 25 to 35°F / -4 to +2°C | **Not cold enough — bugs may survive indefinitely** |
| **Door bins of any freezer** | Adds 5-10°F warmer than internal | Use interior shelves, not door |

### Verify your freezer (one-time, $5-10)

1. Get a refrigerator/freezer thermometer (any hardware store, Amazon, ~$5-10)
2. Place it in the **center** of the main freezer compartment (not door bin)
3. Read after 24 hr stabilization
4. Use the table below to determine your freeze-time requirement

### Freeze time by measured temperature

| Measured temp | Time to kill bugs + eggs |
|---|---|
| -20°F / -29°C or colder | **24 hr** |
| -10°F / -23°C | 48 hr |
| **0°F / -18°C** (FDA target) | **72 hr** |
| 5°F / -15°C | 5 days |
| 10°F / -12°C | 7 days |
| 15°F / -9°C | 10-14 days |
| 20°F / -7°C | 2-4 weeks |
| 25°F / -4°C or warmer | **Insufficient — use heat treatment instead** |

If you can't or won't verify with a thermometer, **default to 7 days** in any standard kitchen freezer — that covers most real-world freezer temps with margin.

### Heat treatment alternative (when freeze isn't reliable)

For ovens that can hold 140-160°F (most can, on lowest setting + convection):

1. Spread chilies single-layer on a baking sheet
2. Oven at **140-160°F (60-70°C) for 30 min** · convection if available
3. Cool fully (~30 min) before storing or rehydrating
4. Kills bugs + eggs in one pass

**Trade-off:** slight oxidation of volatile capsaicinoids · color may darken slightly · some Maillard development. Acceptable for ferment use; the master paste rebuilds flavor depth during the 5-7 day cycle. Less ideal for tadka or oil-bloom use where the bright top notes matter.

## 6. Rehydration Method

For dried chilies going into the master paste, sambal, sauces, or any application requiring fresh-style flesh.

### Equipment

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Heatproof bowl (glass or ceramic) | Large enough to submerge chilies + ~2 cups water |
| Hot water (distilled or filtered, chlorine-free) | ~180°F (just off the boil) — chlorine kills LAB needed in ferment |
| Small plate or lid | To weight chilies under the water |
| Kitchen shears | For stemming |
| Gloves (recommended) | Capsaicin oil transfers to skin · burns 4-6 hr |

### Method (~20 min total)

1. **Boil + rest** — bring 2 cups chlorine-free water to a boil · cut heat · rest 30 sec (drops to ~180°F · fully boiling water destroys flavor compounds)
2. **Stem** each dried chili with kitchen shears
3. **Optional de-seed** — shake seeds out over trash · leave some in for body + color, remove all for cleaner flavor (your call · see §7 for trade-offs)
4. **Quick rinse** under cool water to wash off pantry dust
5. **Soak** — chilies in bowl, hot water poured over to cover by 1″, weight with small plate
6. **Wait by chili type:**
   - Kashmiri (thin-walled, long) — **15-20 min**
   - Thai bird's eye (small, thin) — **10 min** (add to bowl 10 min after Kashmiri)
   - Guajillo / ancho / mulato (thick-walled) — 30 min
   - Chipotle / chile morita (heavily dried) — 30-40 min
7. **Pinch check** — pull one chili, pinch between fingers · pliable + plump + no leathery patches = done · if leathery in spots, soak 5-10 min more
8. **Drain through fine mesh** into a clean bowl/jar — **save the soak water** (brick-red, concentrated flavor, perfect brine top-up for the ferment)
9. Gently squeeze each chili over the strainer for any extra liquid
10. Move drained chilies to cutting board · proceed with chop / blend / pack

### Optional flavor upgrade — dry-toast first

For deeper smoky flavor (worth it for the master paste):

1. Heat dry skillet over medium · no oil
2. Toast each Kashmiri 20-30 sec per side until aromatic and slightly puffed
3. Watch for any darkening past medium-brown → pull immediately
4. Cool 1 min, then proceed with rehydration

Adds ~2 min · builds a Maillard note that pairs well with lacto-fermentation. **Recommended for Sheet 37 master paste.**

## 7. De-Seeding — When and Why

| Goal | De-seed? |
|---|---|
| Smooth Sriracha squeeze-bottle texture | Yes — cleaner final, no seed grit |
| Chunky sambal texture | No — seeds add body |
| Maximum heat | No — though heat is mostly in membrane, seeds carry some |
| Cleaner sweet-chili flavor (Kashmiri) | Yes — Kashmiri seeds skew slightly bitter when blended |
| Maximum yield (when using fewer chilies) | No — every gram counts |
| Pre-Vitamix paste | Yes if you want smooth · No if you want texture |

### Volume + weight delta from de-seeding

| Stage (30 dried Kashmiri example) | With seeds | De-seeded |
|---|---|---|
| Dried weight | ~2 oz / 57 g | ~1.5 oz / 42 g |
| Dried whole, loose | ~3-4 cups | ~2-3 cups |
| Rehydrated whole | ~2 cups | ~1.5 cups |
| Rehydrated + chopped | ~1.5 cups | ~1.25 cups |
| Final paste yield | ~1.5-2 cups | ~1-1.5 cups |
| Seed pile removed | — | ~½-¾ cup |

**Don't throw out the seeds** — save in a small jar for: roasted vegetable sprinkle · chili-pepper finishing salt blend · homemade chili oil · tadka with mustard seeds and curry leaves.

## 8. Dried-to-Fresh Conversion (for Master Paste)

When subbing dried for the 1 lb fresh chili line in [Sheet 37](./fermented-chili-garlic-paste.md):

| Goal paste yield | Dried Kashmiri (with seeds) | Dried Kashmiri (de-seeded) | Add for heat (Thai bird's eye dried) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Half batch (~1.5 cups paste) | ~30 chilies (~2 oz) | ~40 chilies (~2.5 oz) | 5-7 chilies |
| **Single batch (~3 cups paste)** | **~60 chilies (~4 oz)** | **~80 chilies (~5 oz)** | **10-15 chilies** |
| Path A Sriracha (~4 cups paste) | ~80 chilies (~5-6 oz) | ~100-110 chilies (~6-7 oz) | 15-20 chilies |
| 2× batch (Sriracha + Combo + Stir-Fry) | ~120 chilies (~8 oz) | ~150-160 chilies (~9-10 oz) | 20-30 chilies |

### Why de-seeded needs more chilies

De-seeding drops the dried weight by ~25-30%. To match the original paste yield, you need 25-30% more chilies. The salt math compensates automatically — it's calculated by REHYDRATED + DRAINED weight, not dried.

---

## 9. Salt Math for Rehydrated Chilies

**Always 3.5% by drained weight, never by dried weight.** Dried weight underestimates the actual mass going into the ferment; using dried-weight salt math under-salts and stalls the ferment.

| Process | Action |
|---|---|
| 1. Rehydrate + drain per §6 | — |
| 2. Weigh drained chilies in grams | Use kitchen scale to 1 g |
| 3. Multiply by 0.035 | That's your salt in grams |
| 4. Mix salt into chopped chili in mixing bowl | Hand-massage 3-5 min for juice release |
| 5. Pack jar | Should have enough brine; top up with saved rehydration water if not |

### Example

| Stage | Weight |
|---|---|
| 80 dried Kashmiri (de-seeded) | ~4.5 oz / 130 g dried |
| After rehydration + drain | ~450 g |
| Salt at 3.5% | **~16 g** (~1 tbsp) |

If you used the dried weight by mistake (130 × 0.035 = ~4.5 g), the ferment would be massively under-salted and likely fail. **Always weigh after rehydration.**

## 11. Critical Rules

- **Always freeze new dried-chili bags 72 hr before pantry storage.** Egg-kill is non-negotiable for long-term pest prevention. Verify your freezer temp; extend time if warmer than 0°F.
- **Visible mold = toss the chili AND consider tossing the bag.** Mycotoxins do not wash, freeze, ferment, or cook out.
- **Musty smell = toss the bag.** Aflatoxin contamination is invisible. Trust the nose.
- **Calculate salt by rehydrated drained weight, never dried weight.** Off by 4× — ferment fails.
- **Save rehydration water.** Brick-red concentrate — use as brine top-up instead of fresh water.
- **Use chlorine-free water** at every step — soak, rinse, top-up. Tap water kills LAB.
- **Wear gloves for high-volume chopping or de-seeding.** Capsaicin oil burns skin 4-6 hr · eyes for longer · concentrated oil from rehydrated peppers transfers more easily than from dried.
- **Don't pour fully-boiling water on chilies.** 180°F (just off boil) — boiling water scalds the surface and locks in bitter notes.
- **Don't soak more than 1 hr.** Waterlogs the chili, dilutes the soak water past usefulness as brine.

## 12. Substitutions Across Chili Types

| Want | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 lb fresh Fresno → dried equivalent | ~60 dried Kashmiri (~4 oz with seeds) + 10-15 dried Thai bird's eye | Match yield AND heat |
| Fresh Fresno → fresh red jalapeño | 1:1 | Interchangeable |
| Fresh Thai red chili → dried | 1:1 by count if fresh-and-dried both small (~2 inches) | Larger fresh → ½:1 dried |
| Kashmiri (color) → smoked paprika | 1 tbsp paprika per 5 Kashmiri | Less heat, more smoke |
| Chile de árbol → Thai bird's eye | 1:1 by count | Both ~50K SHU |
| Cayenne (ground) → red chili flakes | 1:1 | Flakes have more visible color, similar heat |
| Chipotle → smoked paprika + cayenne | 1 Chipotle ≈ 1 tsp paprika + ½ tsp cayenne | Smoke + heat split |
| Anything → habanero | NOT recommended substitute in cube recipes | Habanero is for the midday heat pulse only — too hot for cubes that dose into every meal |

## 13. Pantry Pest Prevention (Going Forward)

After one bug encounter:

1. **Inspect every dried pantry item** — other chilies, rice, flour, lentils, nuts, dried fruit, oats. Pantry pests spread invisibly.
2. **Bag and freeze anything questionable** for 72 hr (or your freezer's required time per §5) before deciding to keep.
3. **Wipe pantry shelves** with vinegar + water — removes pheromone trails that attract more pests.
4. **Replace bag clips** that touched contaminated items.
5. **Switch to airtight glass jars** for all dried goods · plastic bags + cardboard boxes are pest highways.
6. **Buy smaller quantities, rotate faster.** A bag of dried chilies that sits 12 months is bug heaven; one used in 3 months rarely has visible activity.
7. **Pheromone moth traps** in pantry corners ($5 at hardware stores) — catch active flying adults and confirm the infestation is over after cleanup.
