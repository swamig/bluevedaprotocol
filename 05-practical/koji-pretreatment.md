# Koji Pretreatment

The Round 7 fermentation upgrade. **Shio koji** (salty koji paste) and **amazake** (sweet koji rice) run *upstream* of the cube/sear workflow as a pretreatment layer for tofu, chickpea, black bean, and paneer anchors. A 12–48 hr koji rub before the freeze or flash sear cleaves protein into free amino acids (glutamate amplification), produces ergothioneine (a mitochondria-targeting antioxidant), and generates bioactive peptides — most of which survive freezing into the cube and the flash sear.

Pairs with the new **"Pre-Ferment, Then Freeze"** section in [freeze-system.md](./freeze-system.md). The doctrine: ferment, then freeze, then sear — the enzymes do their work on the interior before cold storage halts further degradation, and the surface residue gets wiped off before high-heat cooking.

## What Is Koji?

*Aspergillus oryzae* cultured on steamed rice (also barley or soybeans for miso/shoyu variants). Sold as **dry rice koji** (kome koji) — small, off-white inoculated rice grains — or fresh. The mold secretes a stack of enzymes: **proteases** (cleave protein → amino acids + peptides), **amylases** (cleave starch → maltose/glucose), and **lipases** (cleave fat → free fatty acids). Same fungus that produces miso, shoyu, sake, and mirin.

## Recipe 1 — Shio Koji (the workhorse)

The salt-cure paste that pretreats every protein anchor.

### Base recipe (~500 g · ~2 cups · ~30 marinades)

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Dry rice koji** | 200 g | Cold Mountain or equivalent · break up any clumps with fingers first |
| **Sea salt** (non-iodized) | 60 g | **~10% by total weight** — the preservation + selective-pressure layer |
| **Filtered water** | 250 g (~1 cup) | Non-chlorinated — chlorine kills the enzymes |

### Method (7–10 days · room temp)

1. **Combine** koji + salt + water in a clean wide-mouth glass jar (~16 oz / quart). Stir thoroughly to coat all rice grains.
2. **Cover loosely** with a clean cloth or loose lid — needs air exchange, not airlock. The koji is aerobic and the salt prevents anything else from taking over.
3. **Mix daily** for 7–10 days. Use a clean spoon · press the rice grains against the side of the jar each time to release enzymes.
4. **Check.** Done when:
   - Rice grains break down when pressed with the back of a spoon
   - Aroma is sweet + savory + faintly floral (like raw oats + miso)
   - The liquid is cloudy + slightly viscous
   - Color is off-white to pale yellow
5. **Refrigerate** to halt further enzymatic activity. (Cold doesn't kill the enzymes — it just slows them ~10×.)

### Storage

| Mode | Life | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fridge (sealed jar) | **6 months** | Enzymes stay active but slow · gets slightly funkier over time |
| Mini cube tray (freezer) | **3 months** | Pour ripe shio koji into mini tray · freeze · transfer to labeled bag · thaw 5 min counter to use |
| Counter (after ripening) | Don't — over-ferments within 2–3 days | — |

## Recipe 2 — Amazake (sweet koji rice)

No-added-sugar fermented sweet drink/topping. Amylase converts rice starch to maltose + glucose during the 55–60 °C hold.

### Base recipe (~3 cups · ~20 cube uses)

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cooked short-grain rice** | 1 cup (~200 g cooked) | White or brown · cooled to ~60 °C |
| **Dry rice koji** | 1 cup (~150 g) | Break up clumps |
| **Filtered water** | 1.5 cups · pre-heated to ~60 °C | Non-chlorinated |

### Method (8–10 hr at 55–60 °C)

1. **Combine** rice + koji + warm water in a heat-safe container. Stir to fully integrate.
2. **Hold at 55–60 °C** for 8–10 hr. Two reliable methods:
   - **Rice cooker on Keep Warm** with lid cracked (the crack drops the holding temp from ~70 °C to ~58 °C — check with a probe thermometer the first time)
   - **Yogurt maker** set to 58 °C
3. **Stir** every 2–3 hr if accessible (not required if sealed).
4. **Check.** Done when sweetness is fully developed — taste at hr 6, 8, 10. Texture is a thick porridge · color is off-white · aroma is sweet + faintly fermented.
5. **Cool fully** before storing. Optional: blend smooth for a drink/sauce texture, or leave whole-grain for a topping.

### Use

| Application | Amount | Why |
|---|---|---|
| [Recovery bowl](./recovery-bowl.md) yogurt | 1–2 tbsp | Natural sweetness alternative on non-cacao days · whole-rice maltose vs. refined sugar |
| Pre-workout fast-glucose drink | ¼ cup amazake + ¾ cup water · stir | Maltose/glucose hits faster than fructose · no added sugar · ~3% sugar by weight |
| Smoothies | 2 tbsp | Sweetness + light viscosity |
| Marinade base for fish/tempeh | 2 tbsp + 1 tbsp tamari | Sweet/savory glaze precursor |

### Storage

| Mode | Life | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fridge (sealed jar) | **1 week** | Sugars start to ferment past 7 days · texture turns boozy |
| Mini cube tray (freezer) | **3 months** | 1-tbsp portions · drop into yogurt or smoothie direct |

## Use Pattern — Pretreatment Targets

The protocol's protein anchors and where koji slots in:

| Target | Pretreatment Method | Hold Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Tofu** (frozen, thawed, brined per [Sheet 07](./protocol-tofu.md)) | Rub **1 tbsp shio koji** per slab · all surfaces | **24–48 hr fridge** before flash sear |
| **Chickpeas** (cooked) | Toss 1.5 cups beans with **2 tbsp shio koji + 1 tbsp water** | **12–24 hr fridge** before patties / croutons |
| **Black beans** (cooked) | Toss 1.5 cups beans with **2 tbsp shio koji** | **12–24 hr fridge** before reducing to liquid for anthocyanin cubes |
| **Paneer** (slab, post-press, before freeze) | Rub **1 tbsp shio koji** per slab | **12 hr fridge** · wipe surface · then freeze per [Sheet 34](./paneer-high-protein.md) |
| **Vegan protein patties** | Mix **1 tbsp shio koji** into wet ingredients before forming | Form immediately · 12 hr fridge rest before cooking |

## Critical Rules

- **The salt is in the koji.** Shio koji is already ~10% salt by weight — DO **NOT** add additional salt to whatever you pretreat. Taste-adjust at the end of cooking; you'll almost never need more.
- **Wipe surface koji off before high-heat sear.** The rice-grain residue burns black at flash-sear temperatures (>200 °C). The enzymes have already done their work on the interior — the surface paste is just fuel for char. Wipe with a damp paper towel, then sear.
- **Don't pretreat for more than 72 hr.** Protein over-degrades and texture turns mushy. The sweet spot is **24–48 hr for tofu**, **12–24 hr for beans**. Past 72 hr the proteases run out of substrate and start hydrolyzing structural collagen analogues, which is what produces the mush.
- **Koji is a fungus — store sealed.** Healthy ripe shio koji smells **sweet/savory like raw oats + miso**. If it grows **fuzzy mold on the surface** (green, blue, black, or fuzzy white — distinct from the white rice mycelium of fresh koji), throw it out. If it smells **alcohol-forward or rotten**, throw it out. The 10% salt prevents almost all contamination, but it's not bulletproof.
- **Non-iodized salt + non-chlorinated water.** Both iodine and chlorine inhibit the *A. oryzae* enzymes and can derail the ripening.
- **Amazake holds at 55–60 °C — NOT above 65 °C.** Above 65 °C the amylase denatures and conversion stops. Below 50 °C lacto bacteria take over and you get a sour mash instead of sweet amazake.

## Bio-Signal — Why This Earns the Slot

| Signal | Mechanism | Status in the protocol |
|---|---|---|
| **Umami amplification** | Koji proteases cleave protein → free glutamate. Sears taste 2–3× more savory at the same salt level. The capsaicin/menthol flash-sear synergy now lands on amino-acid-rich tofu instead of plain protein. | New — extends the existing flavor signal stack |
| **Ergothioneine** | Koji synthesizes ergothioneine, a sulfur amino acid antioxidant with **selective mitochondrial uptake** via the OCTN1 transporter. Heat-stable, freeze-stable, survives the cube. | **New bio-signal** — distinct from SAC (black garlic) and allicin (rested garlic) |
| **Bioactive peptides** | Koji-fermented protein generates short peptides with documented ACE-inhibitory + antioxidant activity. Thermostable below ~150 °C; the flash sear lasts <90 sec per side, so the surface peptides survive intact. | New — synergistic with the cube system's antioxidant load |
| **Lower-allergen profile** | Koji partially pre-hydrolyzes soy + legume proteins, reducing the intact-protein epitope load. Useful for sensitive guts where unhydrolyzed tofu or chickpeas cause friction. | Side benefit |

## Use Cycle — Example Week

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| **Sunday morning** | Shio koji batch (already aging in the fridge from a prior week) — taste-check ripeness · move to active jar |
| **Sunday evening** | Pretreat: rub Monday's tofu slabs · toss Tuesday's chickpeas · toss Wednesday's black beans · all into labeled containers in the fridge |
| **Monday pre-sear** | Wipe surface koji off tofu · flash sear normally |
| **Tuesday** | Pull chickpeas · form patties · cook |
| **Wednesday** | Pull black beans · reduce to anthocyanin liquid · cube |
| **Mid-week** | Start the *next* shio koji batch (~10 days out) — rolling pipeline so there's always a ripe jar |
| **Monthly** | Amazake batch (overnight in rice cooker) · cube → freezer · keep ½ cup fresh in fridge for the week's recovery bowls |

The shio koji jar runs on a ~10-day rolling cycle. Once you're 2–3 weeks in, you'll always have one ripe jar in the fridge and one ripening on the counter.
