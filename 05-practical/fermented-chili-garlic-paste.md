# Fermented Chili-Garlic Master Paste

The master 1-week lacto-ferment that lives in the fridge and gets pulled from weekly for **three downstream finishes** — Sriracha, Heat+Umami Combo Cubes, and Stir-Fry Pulse Sauce. One ferment cycle, three weekly outputs. The 5-7 day wait happens once; every downstream finish from that point is same-day work.

Companion to [freeze-system.md "Pre-Ferment, Then Freeze"](./freeze-system.md) — the protocol's general rule that ferments concentrate signal density before the freeze chain locks it in. This sheet is the canonical implementation of that rule for the chili-garlic axis.

## Why a Master Paste?

| Lever | Single-recipe approach | Master-paste approach |
|---|---|---|
| Ferment cycles per month | 3 (one per finish) | 1 |
| LAB strain diversity per jar | Low (small volume, narrow microbial niche) | High (1 lb peppers + full garlic head = wider succession of Leuconostoc → Lactobacillus → Pediococcus) |
| Capsaicin + allicin precursor concentration | Diluted across three smaller jars | Concentrated in one ~3-cup master pool |
| Decision load weekly | Pick recipe → wait 5-7 days → make | Pull from fridge → finish in 5 min |

The master paste is the upstream. The three finishes are the downstream. The architecture mirrors the cube system: build the dense reduction once, portion the finishes from it.

## Base Ferment Recipe (single batch · 1 lb peppers · ~3 cups paste)

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Red Fresno OR red jalapeño | 1 lb (~450 g) | Stem · **don't seed** · rough chop |
| Thai red chili | 4-6 chilies | Optional · ramps heat by ~2× |
| Garlic cloves | 1 full head (~10-15 cloves) | Peeled · whole |
| Sea salt | 18 g (~3.5% by pepper weight) | **Non-negotiable** — drives the ferment + preserves |
| Distilled water (chlorine-free) | to top up | Only if brine doesn't cover solids after pack-down |

## Method (5-7 days)

1. **Pulse coarse.** Peppers + garlic + salt into the food processor — ~10 sec pulse to a coarse paste. **Not smooth.** (See "No Food Processor" workaround below.)
2. **Pack.** Press the paste into a wide-mouth quart jar. Press solids down firmly so the brine rises above them. Leave 1″ headspace.
3. **Top up if dry.** If brine doesn't cover the solids after packing, add distilled water until covered. **Chlorine kills LAB** — tap water is not acceptable.
4. **Weight.** Weight the solids under the brine with a fermentation weight or a small ziplock of water. **Exposed pepper goes moldy.**
5. **Cover.** Cheesecloth + rubber band, OR airlock lid, OR loose mason-jar lid (burp daily).
6. **Ferment.** Room temp (65-75 °F ideal) for **5-7 days.** Burp daily if using a tight lid — CO₂ builds and will crack the jar by day 3.
7. **Check.** Day 5-7: bubbles rise when stirred; aroma is funky-fruity, not vinegary-yet. Color deepens from bright red toward brick-red.
8. **Halt.** Refrigerate. The master paste lives in the fridge **6 months**.

### No Food Processor — Three Workarounds

The 10-sec coarse pulse is a convenience step, not a chemistry step. Ferment chemistry doesn't care how the paste was reduced.

| Method | Active time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Knife chop + salt massage** (recommended · zero equipment) | ~15 min | Rough-dice peppers ¼″ · smash + chop garlic · combine in bowl with salt · hand-massage 3-5 min until juice releases · pack jar. Produces a superior ferment vs machine — uneven texture creates more microbial niches. |
| **Vitamix on pulse** | ~10 min | Pre-cut peppers to 1″ chunks · work in 2 batches (4 cups raw max per batch) · 4-6 short pulses on Low (½ sec each) · stop at chunky-salsa texture · DO NOT use tamper (smooths everything) · combine batches in bowl for the pack. |
| **Box grater** | ~12 min | Grate peppers on largest holes over a bowl (catches juice) · grate garlic on smaller holes or Microplane · stir in salt · pack jar. Gloves strongly recommended — concentrated capsaicin on fingers. |

## Equipment

| Item | Spec | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wide-mouth quart jar | 32 oz, glass, with seal-able lid | Wide mouth makes packing and weighting easier · narrow-mouth jars trap solids above brine |
| Fermentation weight | Glass disc OR small jar-of-water OR zip-bag of brine | The single most important piece of equipment · don't skip |
| Cover | Cheesecloth + rubber band · OR airlock lid · OR loose mason-jar lid (burp daily) | Airlock is set-and-forget; cheesecloth needs daily skim watch |
| Kitchen scale | Reads to 1 g | Salt at 3.5% is a weight rule, not a volume rule — guessing wrecks the ferment |
| Food processor | Standard 7-cup or larger | A blender works for smaller batches but tends to over-puree |

## Day-by-Day Ferment Timeline

| Day | What you should see | What you should do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Bright red paste, no bubbles, clean salty-pepper smell | Pack jar, weight solids, cover, set on counter |
| 1 | Brine clearer, paste still bright | Burp if tight lid · no other action |
| 2 | First small bubbles when jar is moved · faint sour smell | Burp · do not stir |
| 3 | Active bubbling visible · brine cloudier · smell shifts to funky-fruity | Burp · check weight is still pressing solids under brine |
| 4 | Peak fermentation · CO₂ pressure noticeable on burp · color starting to deepen | Burp · skim any kahm yeast film off the surface |
| 5 | Bubbling slows · aroma rounds out · paste is brick-red, not bright red | First taste check — should be tangy, salty, complex (not vinegary) |
| 6 | Steady, slow fermentation · brine has settled | Taste check #2 · ready to halt OR continue to day 7 for deeper funk |
| 7 | Fully developed · minimal new bubbles | Refrigerate · ferment is done |

## Optional Lacto-Boost

| Add | Amount | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Brine from a previous successful ferment (kraut, kimchi, the last master paste) | 1 tbsp | Inoculates with proven LAB cultures · accelerates Day 1-3 culture establishment · **recommended for first-time fermenters** |

Add at the pack step (Method 2), mixed into the paste before pressing into the jar.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No bubbles by day 3 | Room too cold (< 65 °F) OR salt over 4% OR chlorinated water | Move jar to warmer spot · re-weigh salt next batch · always use distilled / filtered chlorine-free water |
| Fuzzy white/blue/green/black patches | True mold · oxygen exposure (solids above brine) · contaminated jar | **Discard entire jar.** Re-sterilize. Use a real fermentation weight next time. |
| Thin white film on surface, no fuzz | Kahm yeast — harmless surface aerobe | Skim with a clean spoon · continue ferment |
| Vinegary smell instead of funky-fruity at day 5 | Acetic acid bacteria took over (oxygen exposure) | Salvageable for Sriracha finish only · master paste won't have full LAB profile · next batch: tighter weight, less headspace |
| Brine bubbling out of jar | Headspace too small · jar lid too tight | Wipe rim · move to a tray to catch overflow · burp 2× daily for next 24 hr |
| Paste turns brown/grey instead of brick-red | Oxidation from exposure to air | Top with thin layer of brine and re-weight · color will not return but flavor is intact |
| Garlic turns blue-green | Sulfur compounds reacting with trace copper or low-acid environment | Harmless · color stabilises by day 5 · flavor unaffected |

## Three Downstream Finishes

| Finish | Add-Ins (per 1 cup master paste) | Outcome | Cube Tray | Freeze Life |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sriracha** | ½ cup distilled white vinegar + 2 Medjool dates (blended with 1 tbsp warm water) | Squeeze bottle (fridge 6 mo) OR mini cube (freezer 6 mo) | Mini ice cube (~1 tsp / cell) | **6 months** |
| **Heat+Umami Combo Cube** | 2 tbsp tamari + 1 tbsp white miso + 1 tsp toasted sesame oil | Mini cube — the heat+umami layer for stir-fries and combo glazes | Mini ice cube (~1 tbsp / cell) | **4 months** |
| **Stir-Fry Pulse Sauce** | 1 tbsp peanut butter + 1 lime juiced + 1 tbsp tamari | Drizzle fresh OR mini cube — Pad-Krapow-style pulse over rice bowls | Mini ice cube (~1 tbsp / cell) | **3 months** |

## Finish Workflow Detail

### Sriracha finish

Transfer 1 cup master paste to the blender. Add ½ cup distilled white vinegar (5% acetic) and 2 Medjool dates that you've pre-blended with 1 tbsp warm water (or sub 2 tbsp brown sugar). Blend smooth — 30-45 sec. **Optional strain** through fine mesh for the classic smooth-bottle texture; skip the strain for rustic. Pour into a squeeze bottle (fridge 6 months) or a mini ice cube tray (freezer 6 months). The vinegar drops pH to ~3.5 — the same preservation target as the original Mode A in [sriracha-homemade.md](./sriracha-homemade.md). **Day-of use:** swap 1:1 for any sriracha line in [Sesame Sauce Cubes](./sesame-sauce-cubes.md), [Tofu](./protocol-tofu.md) glaze, or any meal needing a clean heat drop.

### Heat+Umami Combo Cube finish

In a small bowl, whisk 2 tbsp tamari + 1 tbsp white miso + 1 tsp toasted sesame oil into 1 cup master paste. Miso goes in at room temp — **never heated** — to preserve live LAB. Sesame oil is the flavor finish, not the preservation; in this case it ships in the cube because the cube melts off-heat into the pan and the oil doesn't see direct flame. Pour ~1 tbsp per mini-tray cell. Freeze overnight uncovered; bag and label `Combo · [date] · use by [date + 4 mo]`. **Day-of use:** drop 1 cube + 2 tbsp water into the stir-fry pan during the last 30 sec — this is the canonical heat+umami layer for [veg-oyster-sriracha-combo-cubes.md](./veg-oyster-sriracha-combo-cubes.md) when you want a faster build than the full combo recipe.

### Stir-Fry Pulse Sauce finish

In a small bowl, whisk 1 tbsp natural peanut butter + juice of 1 lime + 1 tbsp tamari into 1 cup master paste. Use immediately as a Pad-Krapow-style drizzle over rice bowls — the peanut butter loosens slightly from the lime juice's water content and gives a glossy emulsion. **OR** portion into mini cube tray (~1 tbsp / cell) and freeze for up to 3 months — note this finish has the shortest freeze life because peanut butter develops rancid notes faster than the other two. **Day-of use:** thaw 1 cube + 1 tbsp warm water in a small bowl, drizzle over the bowl; pairs naturally with [tofu](./protocol-tofu.md), roasted veg, and any rice-based meal. Add a [rested garlic cube](./garlic-chili-oil.md) off-heat alongside for the allicin pulse.

## Critical Rules

- **Salt at 3.5% by weight of peppers — non-negotiable for the ferment.** Same rule as Sheet 23 (Sriracha homemade Mode A). Too little salt = the wrong microbes win and you'll see fuzzy white/blue/black mold. When in doubt, weigh the peppers and multiply by 0.035.
- **Don't seed the peppers.** Seeds and pith carry most of the capsaicin. Seeding cuts heat by ~70% and removes the bio-signaling target. If Fresno + Thai is too hot, drop the Thai chili before deseeding the Fresno.
- **Burp the jar daily.** CO₂ builds up; a tight-lid jar will crack by day 3 if you don't release it. Cheesecloth or airlock lid avoids this entirely.
- **Surface kahm yeast is fine; fuzzy mold is not.** Thin white film on day 4-5 brine = kahm yeast, skim and continue. Blue, green, black, or fuzzy patches = mold, **start over.**
- **Pull master paste with a CLEAN spoon every time.** Every time. Cross-contamination from a used spoon ends the 6-month shelf life and reintroduces spoilage organisms the salt + acid combo can't fight. Dedicate a small spoon to the jar if needed.

## Bio-Signal — Why The Ferment Pays

- **Capsaicin survives the ferment + freeze.** TRPV1 bio-signal stays intact through the cold chain — neither LAB metabolism nor -18 °C touches the capsaicinoid molecule. The cube melts in the pan and the signal lands.
- **Lactic acid is the new post-biotic.** LAB-produced lactic acid survives freezing as a stable small organic acid. It acidifies the gut at intake (favoring beneficial flora), shows antimicrobial activity against transit pathogens, and contributes to the meal's overall acid load that supports iron bioavailability from the legume layer.
- **Allicin precursors preserved.** Fermentation does **not** destroy alliin (the precursor in raw garlic). When the cube hits the pan and is crushed/melted, alliinase can still activate **IF** the paste isn't heated above 60 °C in the cube. For the highest allicin yield in a finished dish, still add the day-of fresh rested garlic from [garlic-chili-oil.md](./garlic-chili-oil.md) — the master paste is a depth layer, not a replacement. The two stack.
- **B-vitamin synthesis.** Wild LAB strains produce folate, riboflavin, and (in some strains) B12 during the 5-7 day ferment. The exact yield is strain-dependent and not measurable at home, but the bonus is real and free.
- **Bioactive peptides + bacteriocins.** Proteolysis of pepper and garlic nitrogen-containing proteins releases short peptides during the ferment; some LAB strains produce bacteriocins that further preserve the paste and modulate gut flora downstream. The longer ferment (closer to day 7) deepens both.

- **Optional upstream of any cube recipe** that calls for "chili paste" or "fermented heat" — sub the master paste 1:1 for commercial sambal oelek, gochujang base, or any plain chili-garlic line. The salt (3.5%) and acid (post-ferment ~pH 4) profile is compatible across the system.

## Use Cycle (weekly cadence)

| Week | Action |
|---|---|
| **Week 1, Sunday** | Make master paste. 5-7 day ferment starts. Jar lives on the counter under cheesecloth. |
| **Week 2, Sunday** | Ferment finished — refrigerate the master paste. Split into 3 portions (~1 cup each). Execute the three finishes in one ~30-min kitchen block. Bottle the Sriracha, cube the Combo, cube the Stir-Fry Pulse Sauce. Master paste in fridge for next week's pull. |
| **Week 3+, ongoing** | Pull from master paste as needed for any of the three finishes. Re-bottle / re-cube on demand. Start the next master-paste batch around week 5-6 so it overlaps with the tail end of the current one — you want zero downtime in the fridge. |

The cadence collapses to: **one ferment cycle per ~6 weeks, three finishes always in the freezer, master paste always in the fridge.**

## Why Make It Yourself

Commercial chili-garlic pastes — sambal oelek, gochujang, Huy Fong chili garlic — are pasteurized post-fill to extend shelf life. Pasteurization kills the LAB cultures that produced the acid and the post-biotic stack. What you're buying is salt + acid + capsaicin + flavor, with the live signal already cooked out. Most also include xanthan gum, potassium sorbate, and sodium bisulfite as backup preservation.

The home master paste keeps the live LAB until the moment a finish is cubed or heated, and even after the cube melts, the lactic acid + bioactive peptides survive intact. The full preservation chemistry — salt at 3.5%, pH below 4 post-ferment, cold storage — replaces every commercial additive with what the ferment itself produces.

## Substitutions

| Original | Substitute | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Red Fresno OR red jalapeño | Red Anaheim, red banana pepper, red cayenne | Anaheim is milder · cayenne is sharper and thinner-fleshed (less paste yield) |
| Thai red chili | Bird's eye, serrano, dried chile de árbol (rehydrated) | All work · dried chiles need a 20-min soak in warm water first |
| Garlic (full head) | Same garlic, ferment-aged ("black garlic") | Black garlic skips the alliin pathway entirely — use only for depth, not for allicin |
| Sea salt | Kosher salt, pickling salt, Himalayan pink salt | Avoid iodized table salt — iodine inhibits LAB |
| Distilled water | Filtered water with carbon filter (chlorine-free) | Tap water is the #1 cause of failed ferments — chlorine is fatal to LAB |
