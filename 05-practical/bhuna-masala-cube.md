# Bhuna Masala Cube

A concentrated onion-tomato-ginger-garlic-spice base, slow-cooked until the fat separates, frozen into ~1 tbsp cubes. One cube replaces 30-40 min of weekday prep for any North Indian dish — bhurji, dal, sabzi, kofta, butter masala, channa masala, egg curry, aloo gobi. The protocol's cube system has, to date, leaned heavily Thai/East-Asian (sesame, oyster, master paste, sriracha, coconut curry, pad thai peanut); this sheet fills the gap on the North Indian side, where ~80% of dishes share the same bhuna'd masala foundation.

Follows the design principles in the [Sauce Cubing Master Guide](./sauce-cubing-master-guide.md) — concentrate-and-reduce, acid-locked (tomato), salt-stable, finishing aromatics held out. Registers in the [Cube Master Inventory](./cube-master-inventory.md) alongside the other sauce concentrates. Cross-links to [Master Freeze System](./freeze-system.md) for the rested garlic stash and ginger paste cubes used in scale-up runs.

## Why "Bhuna" Matters

**Bhuna** is the slow-cook stage where the onion-tomato base reduces until the oil visibly separates from the solids and pools at the edges of the pan. This is the canonical visual cue — water content has dropped enough that the masala will hold up to a 3-month freeze without separating into a watery sauté on thaw. Skip the bhuna and you've made a flavorless onion-tomato pulp; complete it and you've made a freezer-stable concentrate that lands as restaurant-depth masala when it hits the pan.

## Base Recipe (single batch · ~16 mini cubes · 3-month freeze)

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Avocado oil OR ghee | 3 tbsp | Ghee adds traditional depth · avocado oil for higher smoke point |
| Cumin seeds | 1 tsp | Tadka — bloom in the oil first |
| Yellow onion, finely diced | 4 large (~4 cups) | Reduces ~⅔ during bhuna · don't skip the dice size |
| Fresh ginger, minced | 2 tbsp | Or use ginger paste cube from freezer |
| Fresh garlic, minced | 2 tbsp (~6 cloves) | Or use rested garlic cube + 4 fresh cloves |
| Tomatoes, finely chopped OR canned crushed | 4 medium (~3 cups) OR 1 28-oz can | Canned at this scale is cleaner texture |
| Turmeric powder | 2 tsp | Curcumin · piperine multiplier from black pepper |
| Ground cumin | 2 tsp | Adds to the bloomed seeds |
| Ground coriander | 2 tbsp | The volume backbone of any North Indian masala |
| Kashmiri chili powder | 2 tsp | Color + mild heat · see [Sheet 38](./red-chili-management.md) for sourcing |
| Garam masala | 1 tsp | At end · don't bloom (volatiles burn) |
| Kasuri methi (dried fenugreek leaves) | 1 tbsp · crushed between palms | Earthy "restaurant-style" note · added off-heat |
| Salt | 1 tsp | Adjust after the reduce |

## Recommended Bio-Signal Defaults

**These ship in the cube by default** — added during the spice bloom (Method step 5). The protocol assumes you're stacking them.

| Ingredient | Single batch | 4× run | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Black pepper, cracked** | 1 tsp | 4 tsp | Piperine — absorption multiplier with the turmeric |
| **Asafoetida (hing)** | ¼ tsp | 1 tsp | Anti-flatulent · pairs with cumin · classical Indian gut-base ingredient |
| **Black garlic powder** | 1 tsp | 4 tsp | Heat-stable SAC (allicin replacement for the cube) |

## Method (~35 min · the bhuna is the slow part, don't rush it)

1. **Bloom the cumin seeds** in the oil/ghee on medium-low for ~30 sec until they sputter.
2. **Add onions + a pinch of salt** · sauté on medium · stirring every 2-3 min · until deep golden brown (~12-15 min) · do NOT rush this — color = flavor.
3. **Add ginger + garlic** · stir 60 sec until raw smell disappears.
4. **Add tomatoes** · cook on medium 8-10 min · stirring · tomatoes break down completely.
5. **Add turmeric + cumin + coriander + Kashmiri chili + black pepper + asafoetida + black garlic powder** · stir constantly 2-3 min · spices bloom into the oil.
6. **Lower heat to low · bhuna** · stir every 60 sec for 5-8 min until **oil separates from the mass and pools at the edges** (this is the visual cue — non-negotiable).
7. **Off heat** · stir in garam masala + kasuri methi (crushed between palms first) + final salt.
8. Cool fully before portioning.
9. Pour ~1 tbsp per cell into mini ice cube tray · freeze overnight uncovered · transfer to labeled bag.
10. Label: `Bhuna Masala · [date] · use by [date + 3 months]`.

## 4× Vitamix Batch (~64 cubes)

Same recipe at 4× · pre-pulse the onions in the Vitamix to a ~⅛" rice-grain texture (4-6 short pulses on Low, no tamper) · do the bhuna in a 5-qt heavy-bottomed pot · same method, ~50-55 min total active. The pre-pulse saves ~15 min of knife work and produces a more uniform sauté.

| Ingredient | 4× amount |
|---|---|
| Avocado oil OR ghee | ¾ cup |
| Cumin seeds | 4 tsp |
| Yellow onion (Vitamix-pulsed) | 16 large (~16 cups raw, ~5 cups after pulse) |
| Fresh ginger, minced | ½ cup |
| Fresh garlic, minced | ½ cup |
| Canned crushed tomato | 4 × 28-oz can (~12 cups) |
| Turmeric | 2 tbsp + 2 tsp |
| Ground cumin | 2 tbsp + 2 tsp |
| Ground coriander | ½ cup |
| Kashmiri chili powder | 2 tbsp + 2 tsp |
| Garam masala | 4 tsp |
| Kasuri methi | ¼ cup |
| Salt | 4 tsp |

## 8× Quarterly Batch (~128 cubes · two Vitamix runs)

Same as the 4× scaled to 8 onions, 8 cans tomato, etc. · do the bhuna in two parallel 5-qt pots OR sequentially in one pot (combine for cubing). Yield: ~3 months of supply at 1-2 cubes per North Indian meal (~10-14 meals / week). Fills ~4 mini ice trays.

## Critical Rules

- **The bhuna IS the recipe.** If oil doesn't visibly separate from the mass at step 6, keep going · stopping early loses depth and the cube will freeze as a watery onion-tomato pulp instead of a concentrated masala.
- **Don't add kasuri methi or garam masala to the heat.** Both are aromatic blends · their volatiles smoke off above ~200°F. Add OFF-HEAT only.
- **Use canned tomatoes at scale.** Fresh tomatoes are inconsistent in water content · canned crushed gives reproducible results at 4× and 8× batches.
- **Onions must brown before tomatoes go in.** Pink-onion-then-tomato = sweet pulpy mess · brown-onion-then-tomato = proper masala depth. Maillard development on the onion is what carries the cube past the 6-week mark.
- **Acid + salt + reduction is the 3-month preservation stack.** The tomato acid + reduced water content + salt do the work — same lever as the coconut cube ([Sheet on coconut-protein-cubes](./coconut-protein-cubes.md)).

| Dish | Cubes | Pan method |
|---|---|---|
| **Paneer Bhurji** | 2 cubes | See Recipe section below |
| Dal (any lentil) | 2-3 cubes | Stir into cooked dal at the end · simmer 2 min · finish with ghee tadka |
| Mixed vegetable sabzi | 2 cubes | Stir-fry vegetables in oil · add cubes + ¼ cup water · simmer 5 min · veg coats with masala |
| Channa masala | 3 cubes | Drop into cooked chickpeas + ½ cup water · simmer 10 min · finish with cilantro |
| Butter masala / makhani base | 3 cubes + 2 tbsp cream | Melt cubes + cream into a pan · whisk smooth · add protein |
| Aloo gobi | 2 cubes | Roast potato + cauliflower · toss with melted cubes · stir 2-3 min |
| Egg curry (anda masala) | 3 cubes | Melt cubes + boiled eggs + ½ cup water · simmer 5 min |
| Pulao spice base | 2 cubes | Stir into the rice + water before simmering · masala flavors the rice through |

## Paneer Bhurji Recipe (using the cube)

**Serves 2 · ~12 min**

### Ingredients

- 200 g paneer · crumbled with hands (not too fine · pea-sized chunks)
- 2 Bhuna Masala cubes
- 1 tbsp avocado oil OR ghee
- 1 green chili · finely chopped (optional · for fresh heat)
- ½ red bell pepper · diced (optional · adds sweetness + crunch)
- 1 tbsp fresh cilantro · chopped (day-of garnish · NOT in cube)
- Squeeze of fresh lime (day-of)
- Salt + black pepper to taste

### Method

1. Heat oil/ghee in pan · add green chili (if using) · stir 30 sec.
2. Drop in 2 Bhuna Masala cubes + 2 tbsp water · stir until melted into a smooth paste (~90 sec).
3. Add bell pepper (if using) · stir 60 sec.
4. Add crumbled paneer · gently fold to coat with masala (don't break paneer further · maintain texture).
5. Stir 2-3 min on medium-low · paneer absorbs masala color · don't overcook.
6. **Off heat** · stir in fresh cilantro + lime squeeze.
7. Serve immediately with phulka, paratha, or [Protein Methi Thepla](./protein-methi-thepla.md).

### Pairing notes

- Drop a [rested garlic cube](./freeze-system.md) into the pan at step 6 for the allicin pulse (don't melt — fold in off-heat).
- Pairs with curd, masala chai, mango pickle.
- Cube count adjustments: 1 cube per 100 g paneer · scale linearly.

## Regional Style Variants

Brief swap table for traditional regional bhurji/masala styles. Make the default Punjabi base and tweak day-of at the pan, OR make a small parallel batch (~8 cubes) labeled distinctly when you know you'll cook the style repeatedly.

| Style | Tweaks |
|---|---|
| **Punjabi (default above)** | Garam masala forward · kasuri methi · sometimes a tbsp cream stirred in at end |
| **Marwari / Rajasthani** | Add ½ tsp dried red chili powder for heat · sub mustard oil for the avocado oil (smoke point + flavor) · sometimes a pinch of dried mango powder (amchur) for tang |
| **Gujarati** | Add ¼ tsp jaggery powder + ⅛ tsp roasted methi seed powder for the sweet-savory profile · skip kasuri methi |
| **South Indian-leaning (Madras-style)** | Add curry leaves at bloom step + ½ tsp mustard seeds with the cumin · sub coconut oil for the avocado |

## Freeze Stability + Storage

Same 3-month freezer stack as the coconut cube · tomato acid + salt + reduction = full preservation through to 90 days at -18°C. Past 3 months: still safe but loses ~25% aromatic depth (cumin and coriander top notes fade fastest; turmeric and Kashmiri chili color hold longer). Use older cubes in dal or channa masala where you're adding fresh tadka anyway — the new aromatics carry the bowl.

- **Tomato acid** drops pH below ~4.5, locking out spoilage organisms through freeze-thaw.
- **Salt + ground spices** lower water activity.
- **The bhuna reduction** removes free water — the same lever as [Sauce Cubing Master Guide §4](./sauce-cubing-master-guide.md).
- **Oil separation on thaw is fine** — the cube re-emulsifies the moment it hits a warm pan. Stir for 30 sec and the masala is glossy again.
